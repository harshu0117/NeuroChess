## 3. Detailed Analysis

### Neural Engine (Elo: ~1108)
The Supervised Neural model is the most "promising" of the experimental architectures. 
- It managed to secure **1 win** and **17 draws**.
- The high draw rate suggests the model has learned basic defensive structures but lacks the tactical depth to convert positions into wins against the search-optimized Classical engine.
- It performs significantly better than the RL-only approach, indicating that the Supervised Training phase (2M games) provided a much better foundation than the Self-Play RL phase.

### RL & Hybrid Engines (Elo: ~900)
Both the Reinforcement Learning and Hybrid engines performed poorly.
- Both ended with a **3% win rate** (6 draws, 0 wins).
- The fact that the Hybrid engine (which combines the Neural model with Classical search) did not outperform the standalone RL engine suggests a bottleneck in the integration layer or a mismatch between the policy head and the search algorithm.
- The RL model appears to be making major tactical blunders that the Classical engine easily punishes.

## 5. Next Steps & Recommendations

1. **Improve Neural Features:** The current encoder may be losing critical spatial information. Investigate adding piece-square tables or mobility features to the input tensor.
2. **RL Self-Play Scale:** The RL performance indicates that 100 games of self-play is insufficient for learning tactical depth. Increase the self-play volume by at least 10x.
3. **Hybrid Search Optimization:** The Hybrid engine's failure to outperform the base Neural model suggests the Alpha-Beta search isn't effectively utilizing the Neural policy. Fine-tune the move ordering logic.
4. **Dataset Expansion:** The Supervised Training (Maia dataset) is a strong start, but adding high-level GM games (2700+ Elo) might help the model learn more decisive winning patterns.
